Press Run, Halftone Bloom and Cut Stack — 01, 05 and 06 from the directions page — rebuilt as ambient loops rather than one-shot entrances, with every dial exposed. Set the grid, the timing, the stagger order and the easing; drive colour either as a sequence the field cycles through or as a set of weights it draws from at random.

Why a loop stays readable. The contract is the one from the directions page, and it is what makes running forever safe under copy: the band's base is unprinted stock, the colour is laid on it, and the type sits above in ink. Every cell is ink-on-colour or ink-on-cream at every instant. The field palette is therefore blue, red, yellow and stock — ink is deliberately not in it, since an ink cell under ink type is the one combination that would cost a word.

Recolouring is invisible by construction. Every cell runs the same phase — hold, un-print, take its new colour while it is at zero, re-print. You never see a colour change happen, only a cell that comes back different. Nothing animates opacity.

Each rig's settings are live JSON at the bottom of its panel: edit it and press Apply, or copy it out when you land on something. Send me a config and I can build exactly that.

What the dials mean

  • Cycle is one cell's whole round trip. Hold is how much of that it spends printed and still — at 80% a cell is stationary four fifths of the time, which is what separates ambient from busy.
  • Stagger is the gap between neighbours, and Order decides who counts as a neighbour. Diagonal gives a wave, Scan a sweep, Shuffle a scatter that never repeats its pattern.
  • Stock in the palette is unprinted paper, so its weight is really a coverage dial: more stock, more cream showing through, calmer band.
  • Quiet zone freezes the cells behind the text column. Motion continues around the copy and stops under it.

What ambient costs

An entrance is free once it lands. A loop is not — it holds a frame budget for as long as the band is on screen, so all three rigs pause themselves when scrolled out of view and resume on the way back. That behaviour ships with the direction, not just with this page.

Press and Cut are DOM — a handful of rectangles, one transform each per frame. Halftone is a canvas: at cell 30 on a wide band it is roughly 900 dots, which is far past what DOM nodes want to do sixty times a second.

Under reduced motion no loop starts at all. The engine paints one settled frame and stops, so the band is simply a printed field.
