Why this card exists

Three of the corrections in this system came from an accessibility audit, not from taste — and they were scattered across a token file, a decision card and two CSS comments. The rules below are the ones that changed shipped values, so they are the ones most likely to be undone by someone who doesn’t know why the numbers look odd.

The context is a dark bar. Times, rooms and prices get read at arm’s length, at night, by someone holding a drink. That is the design constraint behind every number on this page.

There is exactly one deliberate AA exception in the system: the ACTION button’s cream-on-red label at 4.19:1. It was taken knowingly, it is recorded in the token file, and it is the only one. Anything else measuring under 4.5:1 is a bug.

The test

Would someone act on this string? A time, a room, a date, a price, a capacity, a wifi password — those decide whether you go and where you stand. They sit at 12px minimum.

What may stay at 11px

Category labels, tab names, eyebrows, tags. They are signals, not information — you recognise them by shape and position, you don’t read them to decide anything.

Never shrink to fit

A fact that won’t fit at 12px is a layout or data problem. Split the string, use the name + qualifier field, or drop something else — don’t go to 11px.

Focus is never removed

Not on buttons, not on cards, not on the day chips. The ring is the one place the accent is guaranteed to appear on every screen — which is why the accent lead being theme-aware mattered enough to decide.

Never colour alone

The day-code is wayfinding, not information. Every card also carries its weekday in words — someone who can’t separate pink from red still reads “Thu”.

Reduced motion

A blanket prefers-reduced-motion rule kills every transition and animation. Design the rest state first — and ship the end-state in prerendered HTML so scroll reveals never leave content invisible to a crawler or a reduced-motion visitor.

Prerender in Day

Force prefers-color-scheme: light in the headless renderer so a machine reporting dark mode doesn’t bake Night into shipped markup.

Vietnamese — what it does to the type

Uppercase VI stacks diacritics above cap height, which breaks tight display leading
× line-height 1.05, weight 100 — marks clip and collide ẢNH SẢNG
ĐỘNG ✓ line-height 1.3, weight 200 — the :lang(vi) rules ẢNH SẢNG
ĐỘNG
Line-height 1.3 minimum

On .type-display and every .reg-far heading. Stacked marks (Ả, Ộ, Ấ) sit above cap height — at 1.05 they clip into the line above.

Display steps to weight 200

:lang(vi) .type-display. At weight 100 the hairline strokes of a diacritic disappear at distance; 200 keeps them visible without changing the character of the face.

Proper names as given

.name-block .type-name never uppercases. bảo anh stays lowercase, MIDNIGHT WOLVES stays caps. A performer’s name is theirs.

Never strip diacritics

Not in addresses, not in names, not to make something fit, not in a filename that gets displayed. Đà Nẵng is never Da Nang on a designed surface.
