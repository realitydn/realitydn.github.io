One geometry, four registers

Every button in the system is the same object: 2px ink border, zero radius, flat down-shadow, Montserrat 700 caps at .11em, hover −3px into heavy, active +2px into light. The role changes only the fill. That is deliberate — colour carries the meaning, geometry carries the brand.

The measurement: across the app there are 249 structural buttons and the roles are used 53 / 4 / 2 times. Red pulls its weight; blue and yellow don’t. That is not a rule problem, it’s a deployment problem — see the pass at the bottom.

The four roles

.btn-actionred · imperative · 53 uses

The thing you came to do. Reserve, book, submit, buy. Red is the loudest fill in the palette and the only one that reads as a command — which is why one per screen is the whole rule.

Cream on red, decided 19.08.26 — 4.19:1, a knowing AA exception
.btn-infoblue · navigational · 4 uses

Take me to more of this. Listings, schedules, calendar subscribe, detail pages. Blue is the wayfinding major — it never asks for a commitment, it just moves you.

Blue also owns eyebrows and links — same job, different element
.btn-noticeyellow · offer · 2 uses

Something is on offer or worth knowing. Deals, drink specials, opening-hours changes, one-off notices. Yellow is warmth, not warning — alerts are red.

Pairs with the yellow left-rule notice bar for non-interactive versions
.btn-altpaper · secondary · everything else

Everything that isn’t one of the three. Cancel, back, filters, tabs, toolbars. Paper fill with an ink border, or the inverted ink fill where it needs more presence. This is the default — a button with no role is this one.

Rules

One ACTION per screen. Not per section, per screen. If two things both feel imperative, one of them is navigation or a notice — re-role it.

Coloured fill takes ink — except red. Info, notice and every day-code fill take ink via --on-day. ACTION is cream on red, decided 19.08.26 at 4.19:1: a deliberate exception taken because red reads as a filled ink button at distance. It is recorded in reality-tokens.json under openCalls so nobody silently "corrects" it — and it does not generalise to any other fill.

Role by job, never by prominence. Blue because it navigates, not because it needs to stand out. Reaching for a colour to add emphasis is how the palette turns to confetti.

No role is also a role. .btn-alt is the correct answer for most buttons. 249 of them are structural and should stay paper.

The deployment pass (B3b): blue takes eyebrows, links and calendar-subscribe; yellow takes deals and notices. About a dozen components, no new colours — it just moves the existing majors onto the jobs the role lock already assigned them.
