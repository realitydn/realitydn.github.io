The one rule that governs the set

Every card inherits its colour from the weekday, not from its category. A .d-thu on the card root sets --day and --on-day; border, plate and fallback all read those. Nothing in the family names a hue directly — that is what makes a weekday remap a one-file change.

The day-colour plate is not a placeholder. It is the card. The poster slice paints over it when there is one, and the plate stays visible while the image loads — so an event with no artwork is still a finished object, never an empty box.

Rules

Facts at 12px minimum

Date, time and room on a card are deciding facts read in a dark bar. .cal-bx-d and .cal-bx-m sit at 11px only because they are inside a hero at 12px — on the list card, step them to 12px rather than shrinking the box.

One misregister per screen

The echo belongs to the next-up card only. Applying .cal-hero-wrap down the list turns a signature into wallpaper and doubles the paint cost of a scrolling pane.

Never tint the plate

The plate is a solid weekday colour. Don’t fade, gradient or overlay-scrim it to “help” the overlay boxes — the boxes are opaque paper for exactly that reason.

The border and the plate match

Both are --day. When a slice covers the plate, that 2px frame is the only remaining day signal, which is why it is never --fg here.

Titles clamp, names split

Two lines then ellipsis on .cal-bx-t. If a title is routinely clipping, that is the name + qualifier data fix, not a smaller font.

Don’t nest a scroll

.cal-feed-pane is the only scroll region in the family. A card is a static frame; an overflowing card is a layout bug.

Drift to fix in code

The live site still animates the hero echo — cal-echo-a / cal-echo-b on desynced 9s and 13s alternating loops, with -lg variants above 640px. C1 retired the drift on 18.08.26 in favour of static 13/6 and 6/3. The four keyframes and both animation declarations come out; the transforms stay as the static offsets.
