The contradiction, in one place

The system says “on surfaces, lead with the three majors — blue / yellow / red; the minors play second fiddle” and, in the same palette table, names pink — a minor — as the default accent. Both cannot be true. --accent is the single most-repeated colour signal in the product: the focus ring on every focusable element, every input focus state, the bottom-nav “you are here” bar, and the deeper of the two misregistration prints behind every hero.

The two properties have already resolved it in opposite directions. The website sets --accent: blue with pink as the echo. The app sets --accent: pink with blue as the echo. Neither overrides it per-context anywhere, despite both calling it “swappable”.

The second axis

The day-code is a separate colour system with no rank in it — seven days, seven hues, equal weight — and on public surfaces it is the largest colour area we paint: full card fills on the feed, borders on the week list, the hero border and poster placeholder on event detail, /now, and profile.

Four of the seven days are minors, and they are not the quiet four. Thursday is the most-programmed day of the week and it wears pink, the colour the system rates least important. 53% of all upcoming events inherit a minor.

Lost by forfeit

Blue is a major whose entire semantic job — information — is exercised on four buttons in the codebase. Yellow’s NOTICE role on two. The majors are not losing because the minors shout; they are losing because outside the day-code the majors are barely deployed as colour at all.

Which suggests the fix is not re-ranking the days. The day-code is doing a different job — wayfinding — and it is currently the only job colour does at volume. Give the majors their declared roles back and the day colours read as a coding system rather than as the brand.

4 — The accent question, rendered live

Left: as shipped — pink is the deep print, thrown furthest from the card, with blue tucked under at half the offset. Right: flipped. Same two inks, same offsets; only which one leads.

A · Flat flip

--accent: blue, --accent-2: pink. One line. Puts a major on every focus ring, input, nav marker and lead print, and makes app and site agree at last.

Cost: loses the pink-on-yellow masthead print, which is the best print on the site.

Suggested start
B · Theme-aware accent

Blue by Day, pink after dark — because the overprint rule already says Day paper takes blue / amber / green; night paper takes pink / red / purple.

Cost: the accent stops being one value. First time the token behaves as “swappable” as both repos claim it is.

C · B, plus a masthead exception

Theme-aware everywhere, but the .intro-hl highlight print stays pink regardless — the one place the hot ink earns its keep.

Cost: a named exception to defend later.

5 — Open, for the deeper dive

Is pink the default accent, or a minor? The palette page currently claims both. Whichever way this resolves, that page and this one should say the same thing.

Is the day↔colour mapping load-bearing now? If regulars have learned “Thursday is pink”, remapping costs more than it gains — and the hierarchy has to come from treatment instead of hue.

Should minors and majors differ in treatment, not just hue? e.g. major days take the fill, minor days take border and tab only. Creates rank without touching identity.

Where do blue and yellow go to earn their volume back? Blue: info states, links, calendar-subscribe, “what’s on” eyebrows. Yellow: notices, deals, highlights.

Rank lists still cycle all seven hues by index — leaderboard, podium, scoreboard, bingo, section dots. Rank is a sequence, not a hierarchy; a majors-first ramp would say more with less.
