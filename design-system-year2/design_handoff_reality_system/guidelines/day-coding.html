How it works

Put .d-{weekday} on an ancestor and it sets two variables for everything inside: --day (that weekday’s palette hue) and --on-day (the higher-contrast text colour to sit on that fill). Purple takes paper; every other hue takes ink.

Never hardcode a weekday hue. Read var(--day) so a single class drives the fill, the border and the text together — and so a future remap is one edit, not a search.

Surfaces that read --day

Feed card fills and borders · week-list card borders · the event-detail hero border, title rule and poster placeholder · the date tab · /now · profile · the control-room calendar grid columns and month-jump cells.

Because the calendar is the product, this is in practice the largest colour system on any public surface — larger than the accent, and larger than the semantic roles.

Unresolved — this axis carries no rank

Seven days, seven hues, equal weight. Four of the seven are minors, and they are not the quiet four: measured against 290 upcoming events, Thursday is the most-programmed day of the week and it wears pink — the hue the palette rates least important. 53% of all events inherit a minor.

Nothing in the system reconciles this axis with the “lead with the majors” rule. Both are stated as canon; they pull against each other on every calendar surface. See Canon — Open Questions.
