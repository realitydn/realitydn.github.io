Three decisions · closed 22.08.26

All three are settled. The alternatives stay rendered below because that is what makes a decision record useful later — the chosen option is marked DECIDED, and what was weighed against it is still on screen.

Recorded in tokens/reality-tokens.json under openCalls as G2–G4, all closed. G1 (focus) has its own card at guidelines/focus.html.

G2

Stock landing on an outer corner · neither fix

Canon says stock is a printed colour and is never on an outer edge or corner — there it vanishes against matching stock and opens the silhouette. But cell order is also fixed, and majors, daycode and the short strip all terminate on stock. So on a cream surface the mark's outer corner disappears, and the two rules contradict each other.

UngroundedDecided
majors, on paper
The bottom-right module is stock on cream, so the corner opens. Kept: it reads as ink laid on the page rather than a badge sitting on it, and it is what makes cell-switching legible — a cell pulling to zero at the edge opens the silhouette further instead of punching a hole in a patch.
Option A · ground patchRejected
grounded on paper-shade — option A
Sits on --paper-shade with one module of clear space. Closes the silhouette, but turns the mark into an object on a surface — and it fights the swap animations, which is what settled it. Still available as data-ground="on" for a mark over photography, or for print export. Never automatic.
Option B · re-orderRejected
re-ordered to end on ink
Swap the last two field cells so every mode terminates on ink. Cleaner with no extra surface — but reordering is explicitly not a parameter in the canon, and it forks the DOM renderer away from the print presets.
Decided · neitherThe open silhouette is correct on screen and is not a bug to patch. Canon's outer-corner rule holds for print, where the mark is a solid object on a sheet; on screen the opening is wanted, and it will matter more as the swap animations get pushed. Automatic grounding has been removed from ink-motion.js — do not restore the background-comparison test.
G3

Four hues for the quiz quick-fingers choices · ratified

The speed round gives four options four colours so they can be told apart across a dark room in six seconds. It is the first time a minor takes a job in the UI. Built on the weekday precedent rather than as an exception — the hue identifies a choice, it never states a status — but blue and yellow are majors, and in a choice set they are doing neither wayfinding nor notices.

Option A · four huesDecided
AThuận PhướcBTrần Thị LýCCầu VàngDCầu Rồng
Fixed slot order like the weekday map — A is always blue. Ink text and a 2px ink border on every plate. Letter, colour and words always travel together, so the hue is never the sole carrier. Correct is a filled ink plate; incorrect is an ink strike. No hue ever means right or wrong.
Option B · ink onlyRevert
AThuận PhướcBTrần Thị LýCCầu VàngDCầu Rồng
Letters and position do all the identifying; selection is an ink fill. Nothing to ratify, no precedent set, and it matches every other list in the app. Slower to scan — four near-identical plates at arm's length in a loud room.
Decided · A, scopedRatified for quiz choice sets only. That clause is the load-bearing part of this decision, not the hues: it does not license colour-coding event categories, menu sections, room codes or tags. A weekday hue remains the only other identifying colour in the system.
G4

Slowed product motion, and idle life · ratified

The parameter sets on the motion canon card are poster-scale — a mark read once, at distance, on paper. On screen the same numbers read as a blip. The prototypes slowed the clock and kept the shape, and added idle life so the press never quite stops. Press both buttons and compare.

Canon card timingPoster scale
Nine plates in 540ms. At poster size, read once, this is right. On a phone it registers as a flicker — you see that something happened, not that it printed.
Product timing + idleDecided
Same shape, ~1.3s end to end. Then idle life: every 5–8s one cell lifts and re-prints in its own colour — never a recolour, since a hue changing on a timer would be saying something. Stops off-screen. Watch this one for a while.
Decided · bothScreen timing is canon; the motion card's sets are the print and poster numbers. Idle life is ratified too, as a separate token from the pass timing — so it can be tuned or dropped later without touching arrival. Idle currently re-stamps a cell in its own colour and never recolours; if the swap animations get pushed further, that is the line to revisit.
