The Ink Strip & SquareThe system's second mark: the locked palette printed as a grid. It is not a logo and never replaces the wordmark — it sits beside it, and it is the one element designed to be recoloured per event.CANON · 19.08.26

Definition

A press colour-control strip, drawn honestly: every colour in the palette printed once, in token order, on a square module, with the paper itself counted as one of the colours. Two forms, one grid — the ink strip (2 × 9 modules) and the ink square (4 × 4). The square is the strip folded, not a second mark.

It carries no meaning of its own. It says this is REALITY and this is printed. Everything else it can say — which night, which mood, how many inks the job affords — it says by recolouring, which is the whole point of building a mark out of the palette instead of a picture.

The order never changes

Majors first, in token order — red, blue, yellow — then the neutral pair, then the minors. In the strip that reads top to bottom; in the square it folds into a Z (red top-left, blue top-right, yellow bottom-left) with the field in the fourth quadrant. Anyone who learns one form has learned the other.

Stock is a colour. A void is a move.

Stock is a positive cell, printed as the substrate's own white — cream #fffbf1 on paper, #ffffff on backlit film. It follows the substrate, never the theme, and it is butted to ink as the neutral pair: paper-and-solid side by side is how a control strip states its own contrast range. Never tint it, never fill it, never let a renderer treat it as transparent.

A void is a variation move — a cell left open so the field shows through (a photo, a wall, a colour). The reference mark has no voids. Variations may have up to three; the stock cell always stays printed so the pair still reads.

Placement rule: stock sits on an inner cell. On an outer edge or corner it vanishes against matching stock and opens the silhouette — the mark then has two different outlines depending on the field. Inner placement gives one closed rectangle everywhere.

03 · Forms

Strip · vertical

2 × 9 The reference form. Poster spines, signage, menu edge, app splash.

Strip · horizontal

9 × 2 Rotate −90°; reading order stays red-first. Headers, footers, awning, TV schedule.

Strip · short

2 × 7 Minors dropped, neutral pair kept. The only sanctioned reduction.

Square

4 × 4 Green steps out for stock. Square frames and small sizes.

Square · anchored

Whole neutral pair kept, two minors out. Ink takes the outer corner so the square still closes.

Keylined

Ink rules between cells. Lit, embroidered, or under the size floor only — on paper it over-articulates.

The reduction rule, in one line

Shorten from the bottom, never from the top. The three majors and the neutral pair are the mark; the minors are the mood. A 2 × 7 short strip and a 2 × 9 full strip read as the same object because the top six modules never move. Nothing between 2 × 7 and 2 × 9 exists — there is no 2 × 8.

04 · Modes

Full · 8 colours

full Identity only: street sign, front page, year poster. Says everything happens here.

Majors · 3 + neutral

majors Minors become an ink/stock checker. Real press budgets, newsprint, two-plate runs.

Day code · 1 hue

daycode One weekday hue from day-colours.json + ink + stock. Shown: Thursday. The event mode.

Ink only

ink Alternate ink and stock so the grid still reads. Vinyl, laser, emboss, single-colour merch.

Drop-outs

dropout True voids show the field. Max three, never a band and its neighbour, stock always printed.

On ink

Any mode, Night field, unplated. Purple lifts to #9a4faa; stock reads as a punched chip; the ink cell drops out and the silhouette opens. Decided 19.08.26 — never plate it.

05 · Placement

One form per surface

The frame decides: long edge → strip, square or tiny frame → square. Never both in one composition, and never the square where the strip fits. Exception: a poster may carry the strip while its footer QR carries the square — separate objects.

One mode per eyeline

A full-palette strip beside a day-code square is the one genuinely confusing combination. Pick one mode per surface.

Clear space & gutter

One module on all four sides, and one full module between the mark and any type. Nothing sits inside the clear space, including the wordmark.

Size floor

Full strip: module ≥ 8px (16 × 72). Short strip: ≥ 6px. Square: ≥ 6px (24 × 24). Below that, keyline it or step down a form — never scale past the floor.

Substrate

Stock = the substrate: cream on paper, white on lit film, and on an ink field the mark stays unplated — the dropped ink cell is the Night form. Purple takes #9a4faa on Night and lit. Shadows are exempt on print and lit — a transmitted shadow is a contradiction.

Lit & small keylines

Backlit flats bloom into their neighbours: 3mm ink keylines between cells on any lit face, Day or Night. An ink field separates only the outer edge, never the interior adjacencies.

Never

No radius, no gradient, no outline around the whole mark, no drop shadow on the cells, no rotation other than ±90°, no reordering the majors, no stock on an outer corner, no eighth ink.

Never a logo

It does not replace the wordmark, never carries the name inside it, and never appears alone on a first-contact surface (shopfront, front page, ticket face).

Never a UI job

Its colours are mood, not state. A red band is not an alert. Do not build status, selection or validation on top of it.

Clear space = 1 module, all sides
QR pairing · 1 mark module = 8.25 QR modules

The QR pairing

The mark and the QR are the same object drawn for two readers: a modular grid of ink on stock, hard edges, no radius, mandatory unprinted border, fixed reading order. Our code is v2, 25 × 25 modules with a 4-module quiet zone — 33 modules overall. Set 1 mark module = 8.25 QR modules and the square's outer width matches the code's exactly.

Because the quiet zone is already 4 modules of stock, the mark may sit flush against the code and it still scans. Two hard limits: never restyle the code (no colour fill, no knock-out, no inversion — assets/qr/ is verified decodable from 120px) and never place the mark inside the quiet zone. Everything expressive happens beside the code.

06 · The Studio contract

A generator needs six inputs and nothing else. Renderers that cannot read CSS variables import tokens/ink-strip.json and day-colours.json directly — the same way the schedule and print renderers already do.

form      "strip-v" | "strip-h" | "strip-short-v" | "strip-short-h" | "square" | "square-anchored"
mode      "full" | "majors" | "daycode" | "ink" | "dropout"
day       "mon".."sun"          // daycode only — hue read from day-colours.json
module    number (px | mm)      // floors: strip 8, short 6, square 6
substrate "paper" | "lit" | "ink"   // sets the stock value and the purple value
keylines  0 | number           // ink rule width; required when substrate = "lit"

invariants the tool must enforce
  · cell order is fixed — expose recolouring, never reordering
  · stock is always an inner cell, always printed, never transparent
  · dropout: max 3 voids, never two adjacent, never the stock cell
  · module never below the floor for the chosen form
  · clear space = 1 module, baked into the export bounds
  · export SVG at integer module sizes so cells stay hairline-free
